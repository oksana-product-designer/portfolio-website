From a design sprint workshop to a validated prototype

Primary inbox for accountants is a tool that gathers incoming primary documents from every channel in one place and moves them into accounting software. I took part in the design sprint as a team member, then prepared, ran and synthesized the validation interviews and usability tests.

My role
Sprint participant, UX researcher, product designer
Deliverables
App design, clickable prototype, research report
Team
A product manager, 2 designers, a developer, 2 domain experts, 2 client stakeholders
Timeline
May-July 2026
9interviews with accountants conducted across 7 industries
78%interested in the product, against a 60% target
6.6ease score (SEQ), against about 5.5 in typical studies
7Must-have modules set in the MVP scope
01

Context and challenge

Accountants receive primary documents through several channels and process them in bulk. Volume depends heavily on the company: about 10 documents a month for one, 100 for another, and 200 to 300 for some. One accountant described the routine as collecting a month of payments, then sitting down for three to four days to work through all of it.

“I collect the payments for the month, then I sit down, and it takes three or four days to process all this primary documentation.”

«Я збираю оплати за місяць, потім сідаю і днів три-чотири всю цю первинку опрацьовую»

Hypothesis

If we present a tool that collects primary documents from different channels and loads them quickly into accounting software, more than 60% of the accountants we interview will want to try it.

03

Pre-workshop research

Before the workshop, the team gathered three kinds of input so the sprint started from evidence rather than assumptions.

Domain research findings

  • Accounting runs in overlapping monthly, quarterly, and yearly cycles, so deadlines compete.
  • Work differs by industry (IT, trade, manufacturing, agro), so no single process fits all.
  • BAS/1C, MEDOC, and Excel cover most tasks, yet the role also spans HR and banks.

User interviews findings

  • Verification is constant: accountants re-check what systems generate before moving on.
  • Late or missing primary documents, often on paper, are the main blocker to closing a period.
  • They need confidence that information is safe to apply and a buffer before deadlines.
04

Design sprint workshop

The team ran a design sprint workshop. I took part as a team member rather than as the facilitator, so the section below focuses on what the team produced and what carried forward into the prototype.

We developed a concept for an accounting tool to manage primary documents, plus a storyboard of its core features and user journey. These will guide future designs and testing with potential customers.

05

From sprint output to MVP scope

The workshop output became an app design and a clickable prototype. The scope was framed as one end-to-end scenario, so every module in the MVP serves the same path from receiving a document to handing it over to accounting.

06

Research and validation

The part of the project I owned end to end

Method

Each session was a hybrid moderated format: an interview about how the accountant handles primary documents today, followed by a usability test on the clickable prototype. I wrote the script, moderated the sessions and synthesized the results.

9interviews
7different industries
5chief accountants

Idea validation: hypothesis confirmed

Interest cleared the 60% target. Five of nine interviewees gave the top score of 5. A sixth showed equally strong interest without giving a number, and a seventh gave a 4. One was conditionally positive and one was cautiously skeptical.

Price signal

Risks to design around

Recognition accuracy

“Even if it does enter the items, the question is whether it enters them correctly.”

Transfer into accounting

“It’s very important to me that the chain of documents is built in the accounting database.”

Distrust of cloud storage

One team lost documents in a service at the start of the war in 2022, and has duplicated to a drive ever since.

07

Outcome and reflection

Impact

The client took the validated concept into development and used the findings to prioritize the MVP scope.

What I would change

Hints given during usability tests could inflate task success. Next time I would agree on when and how to hint before the sessions and record hinted tasks separately.

What I learned

Validation fits inside a sprint when the scope is narrow. A prepared script and a clickable prototype are enough to turn assumptions into evidence within days.
